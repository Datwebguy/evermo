# Evermo — idea post (thread draft)

---

every airdrop, loyalty program and game has rules that decide who gets what. 🧵

the problem: you usually can't check them, and they can change after you've done the work.

---

imagine you spend 6 weeks being active on a project for its airdrop.

on drop day, the rules are different. the criteria moved. there was an admin key the whole time.

you have no proof of what the rules were, and no way to hold anyone to them.

---

projects have the opposite problem.

they say "the rules are fair and final". users have no reason to believe it.

---

my idea: evermo

rules that last forever.

evermo turns any app rule into a permanent circuit on @TapeOutWorld, on @XLayerOfficial.

once it's taped out, nobody can change it. not the project, not evermo, not anyone.

---

how it works

-> describe
the project writes its rule in a simple form. "active 4+ weeks and 10+ transactions → tier 2". no hardware knowledge needed.

-> build
evermo turns it into the smallest NAND circuit, shows how many gates it uses and what it costs, and tests every possible input before anything goes on chain.

-> tape out
one click, on x layer. the rule becomes a circuit nobody can edit.

-> use
the project's contract asks the circuit for every decision.

-> verify
every rule gets a public page on DeWEB. anyone can read it in plain words and test any input against the real on-chain circuit. no wallet, no account.

---

why this works

a tapeout circuit can't be upgraded. there's no admin key on it.

checking it is a free read, so anyone can do it.

and the evermo app itself lives on DeWEB, so nobody can swap the page you're looking at.

---

who it's for

-> projects running airdrops, loyalty programs, games and votes that want users to trust them
-> users who want to check the rules before they put in the work
-> developers who want a permanent rule with one call

---

how evermo earns

evermo runs its own tapeout processor. every rule taped out through evermo uses evermo transistors, and the processor earns a share of every mint.

rules are permanent, so every new campaign or season needs a new rule.

no servers, no user funds, no token sale.

---

what it can't do (being honest)

-> the circuit locks the logic. the project still supplies the inputs, so the project should lock its contract to the circuit too.
-> rules have to be small: a few inputs, not lists of thousands of wallets.
-> a wrong rule is wrong forever. that's why evermo tests every input before tape-out.

---

the rule is simple:

if a rule matters, put it in a circuit. then nobody can change it, and everybody can check it.

evermo is a proposed idea for tapeout idea scouts. early circuit tools exist; the full product isn't built yet.
